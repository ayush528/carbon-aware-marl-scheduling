The problem is not “where is carbon lowest”

Three facts make the obvious answer untrustworthy — and they are what the work has to survive.

1 · The region is missing

Public workload traces (Azure, Alibaba, Google) never name the grid region the machine sat in. Every “carbon-aware” result therefore rests on an unverifiable mapping.

Inspected — 29 trace families

2 · The forecast is wrong, sometimes a lot

Grid carbon forecasts miss by tens of g/kWh during ramps. A scheduler that trusts the point forecast moves work on noise and can emit more.

Verified — NESO forecast vs actual

3 · Moving work costs something

Migrating or deferring a task risks its deadline. Carbon saved by missing SLAs is not a result.

Verified — 27–48% SLA violations in the shipped RL agents

So the contribution is not a new scheduler. It is a defensible measurement setup: region-matched ground truth, calibrated uncertainty, and deadlines priced as risk.

What the data does not give us

Stated up front, because a reviewer will find them anyway — and each one is handled, not hidden.

Regional actuals do not exist

NESO's regional endpoint ships forecasts only (0 actuals across the full matrix, re-verified for 2024; the bulk all-regions endpoint too). Regional actuals are estimated as

actual_region = actual_national × forecast_region ÷ forecast_national

This is an estimator with a stated identity, used consistently for bands, simulator inputs and evaluation. Not a measured regional actual. Assumption

A real outage, left visible

NESO has 15 missing hourly slots on 11–12 Jun 2024 in every region (8769 of 8784). The simulator indexes carbon positionally, so our installer forward-fills exactly those and records the count in a manifest. No other interpolation anywhere.

Verified — counted in install_manifest.json

The price is national

GB wholesale price does not vary by region, so the “cheapest region” controller has nothing to differentiate and is reported as degenerate (+3.1% carbon from tie-breaking only). It is not a finding about prices.

Verified — identical across sites

One AWS region for GB

SustainCluster prices inter-site transfer through an AWS region matrix; GB has exactly one (eu-west-2), so the transmission-cost term is 0 and inoperative. Never read that as evidence about transfer cost.

Verified — matrix diagonal

Uncertainty: conformal bands, not error bars

Distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee: P(actual ∈ band) ≥ 90%, with no assumption about the forecast error shape.

Method

q = sorted(|actual − forecast|)[⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉ − 1]
  • Calibrate on a window the evaluation never sees.
  • Band = forecast ± q; for regions, q scales with the region's forecast.
  • Deterministic, stdlib only — re-running reproduces the same numbers.

Now being replaced by the stronger design

A single Jan→Feb split proves the method can hit target once. The scheduler must calibrate from the past only, so exp010 re-runs 2024 walk-forward: every month is calibrated on the 28 days before it, giving 168 (region × month) cells instead of one. It also reports how often the band makes the top-two ranking certain — the number a risk-averse policy actually consumes.

Why uncertainty changes the decision

A band cannot reorder regions — it raises the bar for acting. That bar is measurable.

Two sites, one uncertain forecast

Routing work to A is only defensible when A's upper bound is below B's lower bound. The gaps overlap here — a point-estimate rule would still move.

What we report

move only if f_clean / f_dirty < (1 − q) / (1 + q)
  • Threshold ratio per month — how big a carbon gap must be.
  • Band-robust share — on what % of slots the best-vs-runner-up ranking survives the band.
  • Median best/second ratio — how far the real data sits from that bar.

With q ≈ 0.09 the bar is ratio < 0.83: a scheduler needs the clean site to be at least ~17% greener before uncertainty lets it commit. That is the difference between “looks cheaper” and “is cheaper”.

Why the shipped RL agents are the motivation

All four shipped SAC checkpoints were evaluated with the same harness:

  • None beats the best rule baseline on carbon.
  • All four violate 27–48% of task SLAs.
  • Root cause found in their reward config: the timeliness penalty is commented out — the agent is never told deadlines matter.

That is the gap this project fills: a learned policy that is explicitly paid for timeliness, and charged for risk, not just for carbon.

Risk-aware objective + limited communication + calibrated bands, on real region-matched UK carbon. This combination is the novel part.

What an agent sees, does, and is paid for

Fixed now so the ladder is comparable; only the policy changes between rungs.

Observation (per site)

  • Own queue depth, running tasks, GPU utilisation
  • Own carbon forecast and its band (± q, in g/kWh)
  • Own electricity price
  • Deadline slack of queued work (min / mean / count at risk)
  • Messages: one scalar per neighbour — its marginal carbon and queue pressure

Limited communication is a hard constraint: O(n) scalars, not the global state.

Action

  • Route an arriving task to a site (including local)
  • Defer if the deadline allows and the forecast is bad
  • Admit / hold when the local queue is saturated

Discrete, per-site, executed locally — no central dispatcher at runtime.

Objective

min E[CO₂] + λ·E[cost]
+ μ·CVaRα(SLA violation)
  • Carbon from real regional intensity
  • CVaR: average of the worst α tail of deadline misses — not just the mean
  • Bands feed the observation; CVaR prices the tail

Deadlines are a constraint, not a tie-breaker — the failure mode of the shipped agents.

Why CVaR and not a hard constraint

A carbon-aware scheduler faces a fat tail: one bad forecast during a ramp can strand work. A mean-penalty objective happily trades a rare catastrophic miss for a small average gain. CVaR prices that tail explicitly, so the learned policy becomes conservative exactly where the data is uncertain — the same place the band is wide. That coupling is the mechanism worth testing.

The claim, and what is actually new

Verified against a 30-paper survey (2024–2026, DOIs checked): the individual pieces exist. The combination does not.

Four parts, none of them new alone

  • Decentralised MARL with limited communication
  • Calibrated forecast uncertainty (conformal)
  • Real region-matched carbon (NESO, 14 DNO regions)
  • Risk-aware deadlines (CVaR on SLA)

What the paper claims

First demonstrated combination of those four, evaluated on region-matched ground truth, with uncertainty as a measured input rather than an assumed noise term.

  • All savings are relative to identical demand.
  • Absolute numbers labelled illustrative.
  • Region mapping disclosed in the abstract, not a footnote.
  • Claims kept inside the EuroSys-2024 envelope.

The discipline that makes it defensible

Every design choice protects one property: the uncertainty is measured, never assumed. A band is calibrated only on data the evaluation cannot see; a simulator result is accepted only if the simulator's own accounting reproduces the input series; a data gap is estimated with a stated identity or declared missing. Anything that would quietly strengthen a number is rejected.

Limits we will state in the paper

Known, quantified where possible, and none of them hidden in a footnote.

  1. Regional actuals are estimated. NESO ships no regional actuals; the national-scaling identity is used everywhere. A reviewer can recompute it exactly.
  2. The 7-day window is short. One week cannot support variance claims. Multi-window, multi-seed runs are planned before any headline number.
  3. Transmission cost is inoperative for GB. One AWS region ⇒ matrix diagonal ⇒ cost 0. Not a finding.
  4. Price is national. The price-only controller is degenerate by construction.
  5. Zero-forecast slots exist (~4.9% of 2024 regional slots). Under national scaling they are trivially “covered”, so coverage is reported both on all slots and on informative (positive-forecast) slots.
  6. Region mapping is an assumption. No public trace names its grid region; the four-site mapping is declared, not derived.
  7. Deadlines are the simulator's model, not measured from a real LLM serving fleet.
